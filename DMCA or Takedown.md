# HyperZHub Takedown Policies

**Last Updated:** November 5, 2026  
**Applies To:** HyperZHub and its associated services, resources, pages, and infrastructure, to the extent controlled by HyperZHub.

This document establishes the procedures for submitting, reviewing, and responding to takedown requests concerning HyperZHub.

It contains two separate categories:

1. **DMCA Copyright Takedowns**
2. **General Takedown Requests**

Each category has its own submission requirements, verification procedures, evidence standards, and review process.

Submitting a request does not guarantee that HyperZHub will grant it. HyperZHub evaluates requests according to their legal basis, supporting evidence, scope, authenticity, and applicable law.

**Important distinction:** Additional information requested by HyperZHub is intended to support verification and review. It does not replace statutory requirements, create additional legal prerequisites, or override a binding legal obligation.

---

# CATEGORY 1: DMCA COPYRIGHT TAKEDOWNS

## 1.1. Purpose and Scope

This category applies to complaints alleging that copyrighted material is being infringed through material accessible on or through HyperZHub.

Copyright complaints must identify the protected work, identify the allegedly infringing material, explain the claimed infringement, and include the applicable declarations and contact information.

HyperZHub does not automatically accept allegations of copyright infringement as established facts.

## 1.2. Submission Contact

Copyright complaints may be submitted to:

- hyperzteam@proton.me
- hyperzteam455@gmail.com

**Required email subject format:**

`DMCA COPYRIGHT COMPLAINT - HYPERZHUB`

If the request concerns multiple separate works or multiple unrelated resources, organize the complaint into clearly numbered sections.

## 1.3. Requester Identity and Contact Information

Provide the following information to the extent applicable:

### A. Personal identification

1. Full legal name.
2. Preferred professional name, if different.
3. Role in the complaint.
4. Whether you are the copyright owner or an authorized representative.
5. Country of residence or relevant jurisdiction.
6. State, province, or region, if applicable.
7. Preferred contact email.
8. Additional contact email, if available.
9. Telephone number, including country code.
10. Mailing address.
11. Preferred method of communication.
12. Time zone and reasonable contact hours.

### B. Organization information

If submitting on behalf of a company, organization, or other legal entity, provide:

1. Full legal name of the entity.
2. Entity type, if known.
3. Country and jurisdiction of registration, if applicable.
4. Official website.
5. Official business or organizational email.
6. Registered or principal business address.
7. Public telephone number, if available.
8. Department responsible for the complaint.
9. Your official position or title.
10. Name and position of the person supervising or authorizing the complaint, if applicable.
11. A description of the relationship between the entity and the copyrighted work.

An individual copyright owner is not required to represent a company or organization.

### C. Representative authorization

If you are not the copyright owner, explain:

1. The identity of the copyright owner.
2. Your relationship to the owner.
3. Whether you are an employee, attorney, agent, representative, or other authorized party.
4. The date your authorization became effective, if known.
5. The scope of your authority.
6. Whether your authority includes submitting copyright complaints.
7. Whether your authority includes requesting removal or disabling of access.
8. Whether your authority covers the particular work identified in the complaint.

Provide a copy or reasonable confirmation of authorization when available. Do not submit confidential agreements or unnecessary personal information when a redacted confirmation would suffice.

## 1.4. Copyrighted Work Identification

Identify every copyrighted work involved.

For each work, provide:

1. Official title or name.
2. Type of work, such as software, artwork, photography, video, writing, music, or other protected material.
3. Name of the creator.
4. Name of the current copyright owner.
5. Original publication date, if known.
6. Original publication location.
7. Original website or source URL.
8. A description of the work's original content.
9. The specific elements claimed to be protected.
10. Whether the work is registered with a copyright office, if applicable.
11. Registration number, if available.
12. Registration jurisdiction, if applicable.
13. Registration date, if available.
14. Any relevant licensing information.
15. Whether the work has been assigned, transferred, or licensed to another party.
16. Whether another person or entity has an ownership interest.
17. Whether the work is jointly owned.
18. Whether the complaint covers an entire work or only a specific portion.

### Supporting ownership evidence

Where available, include relevant supporting material, such as:

- Copyright registration records.
- Original publication records.
- Original project files or creation records.
- Dated source material.
- Licensing agreements or ownership statements.
- Assignment or transfer documentation.
- Official portfolio or publication pages.
- Other documentation reasonably supporting the claimed ownership.

A copyright registration is not universally required to submit a DMCA notice. These supporting materials are requested to assist with verification and are not a substitute for the statutory notice requirements.

## 1.5. Exact Identification of Allegedly Infringing Material

For every allegedly infringing item, provide:

1. Exact HyperZHub URL.
2. Exact page, file, resource, or feature name.
3. The specific location within the resource.
4. A description of the material being challenged.
5. The exact copyrighted work it allegedly copies or infringes.
6. An explanation of the similarities.
7. The approximate date the material was discovered.
8. Whether the material is currently accessible.
9. Whether the material is hosted directly by HyperZHub or linked through an external provider, if known.
10. Any relevant alternate URLs.
11. Screenshots showing the identified material.
12. Any additional information necessary to locate it.

For multiple URLs, provide a separate numbered entry for every location.

Do not rely exclusively on a homepage URL, domain name, search result, or general description when a specific location can be identified.

## 1.6. Explanation of the Alleged Infringement

For each identified item, explain:

1. What protected material is allegedly being used.
2. How the material is allegedly reproduced, distributed, displayed, or otherwise used.
3. Why you believe the use is unauthorized.
4. Whether permission was previously granted.
5. Whether a license exists.
6. Whether the license has expired or been revoked, if applicable.
7. Whether the material is being used under an exception or limitation that you have considered.
8. Why you believe the relevant use is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law.
9. Whether you previously contacted the person responsible for the material.
10. Whether the material has been removed, changed, or relocated elsewhere.

If the complaint concerns only one component of a larger page or resource, identify that component instead of treating the entire resource as infringing without explanation.

## 1.7. Requested Action

For each identified URL, specify:

1. The exact action requested.
2. The specific material to be removed or disabled.
3. Whether you seek removal of a file, page, asset, or other identified component.
4. Whether you seek disabling access to a particular location.
5. Whether you believe a broader restriction is necessary.
6. The factual and legal explanation supporting that broader scope.
7. Whether a narrower action would address the alleged infringement.
8. Whether the complaint concerns additional resources outside the listed URLs.

A complaint concerning one resource does not automatically justify removal of unrelated resources, disabling the entire HyperZHub website, or disabling the entire domain.

Any request for broader action must be independently explained and supported.

## 1.8. Required Copyright Declarations

A formal DMCA notice should include the declarations required by applicable law, including:

1. A physical or electronic signature of the copyright owner or a person authorized to act on the owner's behalf.
2. Identification of the copyrighted work claimed to have been infringed, or a representative list where legally permitted.
3. Identification of the allegedly infringing material and information reasonably sufficient to locate it.
4. Contact information reasonably sufficient to reach the complaining party.
5. A statement that the complaining party has a good-faith belief that the challenged use is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law.
6. A statement that the information in the notification is accurate.
7. The required statement under penalty of perjury that the complaining party is authorized to act on behalf of the owner of the exclusive right allegedly infringed.

These statutory elements control where applicable. Additional verification requests do not replace or override them.

## 1.9. Additional Supporting Documentation

To help HyperZHub assess the complaint, submit any additional relevant documentation available, such as:

- Original work links.
- Archived publication records.
- Ownership records.
- Relevant license terms.
- Permission or authorization records.
- Correspondence concerning the disputed use.
- Screenshots with dates and source URLs.
- A comparison identifying the allegedly copied elements.
- Relevant registration or assignment records.
- A list of other affected locations.
- A chronology of relevant events.

Do not fabricate evidence, alter screenshots to misrepresent the original material, or submit documents that you do not have authority to disclose.

## 1.10. Incomplete, Misleading, or Abusive Complaints

HyperZHub may request clarification or additional supporting information where appropriate.

Examples of issues include:

- Unidentified or incorrectly identified material.
- Unclear ownership claims.
- Unverified representative authority.
- Conflicting ownership statements.
- URLs that do not lead to the disputed material.
- Claims that do not explain the alleged infringement.
- Requests that extend beyond the identified material without justification.
- Evidence that appears altered or misleading.
- Claims made by a person who has not established authority to represent the owner.

Knowingly making material misrepresentations in a copyright notice may have legal consequences, including under applicable provisions of the DMCA.

## 1.11. Counter-Notifications

Where applicable, a counter-notification must contain the information and declarations required by applicable law.

A counter-notification should identify the removed or disabled material and its former location, include the required signature, provide the required statements, and include the required contact and jurisdictional information.

HyperZHub will evaluate counter-notifications according to applicable law and any relevant statutory procedures.

## 1.12. No Automatic Whole-Domain Removal

HyperZHub does not treat a complaint concerning an individual copyrighted work as automatic justification for removing an entire domain or unrelated service.

A broader request must identify the additional material involved and explain the independent basis for the requested scope.

## 1.13. No Admission of Liability

Receiving, reviewing, investigating, acknowledging, or acting upon a complaint does not automatically constitute an admission that infringement occurred or that HyperZHub accepts the complainant's legal interpretation.

## 1.14. Reservation of Rights

HyperZHub reserves the right to review complaints, request additional information, assess the scope of the allegations, dispute unsupported claims, and take actions permitted by applicable law.

- Schools and school districts.
- Educational institutions.
- Employers.
- Businesses.
- Government agencies.
- Law enforcement agencies.
- Attorneys and legal representatives.
- Hosting providers.
- Domain registrars.
- Infrastructure providers.
- Website owners.
- Individuals.
- Other organizations or interested parties.

A requester's position, title, organizational affiliation, or claimed authority does not automatically establish a legal right to demand removal.

8. Whether the request is based on an existing order or an independent legal claim.
9. Whether any relevant exceptions or limitations have been considered.
10. Whether the requested action is mandatory or merely being requested voluntarily.

If relying on a court decision, administrative decision, or formal legal interpretation, identify the document and provide a copy or official reference where available.

A legal citation without an explanation of its relevance may be insufficient to establish the basis for a voluntary takedown request.

## 2.9. Special Requirements for Schools and Educational Institutions

Schools, districts, teachers, administrators, and educational organizations must identify their institutional role and explain the basis of their request.

Provide:

1. Full legal name of the school or institution.
2. District or governing organization, if applicable.
3. Official institutional website.
4. Official institutional email address.
5. Physical institutional address.
6. Public telephone number.
7. Name of the requesting employee or representative.
8. Official job title.
9. Relevant department.
10. Name and title of the supervising administrator, if applicable.
11. Confirmation that the requester is authorized to speak for the institution.
12. The specific HyperZHub URLs involved.
13. The exact conduct or material being challenged.
14. The specific institutional, contractual, or legal rule being relied upon.
15. The full text or official reference for that rule, where available.
16. An explanation of how the rule applies to the specific material.
17. Whether the request concerns school-managed devices or networks.
18. Whether the request concerns students, employees, or the general public.
19. Whether the request is intended to apply only within the institution.
20. Whether a narrower restriction would address the stated concern.
21. Any supporting records or formal authorization.
22. The exact action being requested.

### Institutional authority and limitations

A school may establish and enforce rules concerning its own networks, managed devices, accounts, and educational environment.

However, internal school rules do not automatically become binding requirements for an independently operated website.

A school requesting removal from HyperZHub should explain the legal or other independently applicable basis for that request.

The following statements, without additional supporting authority, do not independently establish a legal obligation for HyperZHub to remove an entire website:

- "Our school blocks this website."
- "Our district does not allow this website."
- "Students are not permitted to visit this website."
- "The website violates our internal acceptable-use policy."
- "Our administration wants this website removed."
- "We do not approve of this website."
- "Our network provider has restricted access to this website."

These statements may explain the institution's position, but they do not independently establish authority over an unrelated website.

Nothing in this section prevents an institution from enforcing its own policies on its own systems or complying with applicable law.

## 2.12. Requests Based on Contracts or Ownership

If a requester relies on a contract, license, property right, or other ownership interest, provide:

1. The identity of the parties.
2. The relevant agreement or ownership record.
3. The effective date.
4. The relevant provision.
5. The alleged breach or violation.
6. The relationship between the agreement and HyperZHub.
7. Evidence that the requester is a party or authorized representative.
8. The specific material affected.
9. The action requested.
10. Any relevant notice or dispute-resolution provisions.

Do not submit unrelated confidential contract material. Relevant excerpts and redacted copies may be provided where sufficient.

## 2.13. Proportionality and Whole-Domain Requests

HyperZHub distinguishes between requests concerning:

- A specific file.
- A specific page.
- A specific resource.
- A particular feature.
- A particular service.
- The entire website.
- The entire domain.

A request concerning one resource does not automatically justify a restriction affecting unrelated resources or the entire platform.

Any request seeking a whole-domain or whole-service restriction should separately explain:

1. Why the entire domain or service is involved.
2. Why the alleged issue cannot be addressed through a narrower action.
3. Which additional resources are affected.
4. The legal or contractual basis for the broader action.
5. The evidence supporting the broader scope.
6. Whether the requested restriction is mandatory.
7. Whether the request applies to all users or only a particular group.
8. Whether the restriction is temporary or permanent.

HyperZHub may evaluate the scope of a request independently from the merits of the underlying complaint.

## 2.14. Requester Declarations

A general takedown request should include a signed declaration confirming, to the requester's knowledge:

1. The requester has identified themselves accurately.
2. The requester has accurately described their organization and role.
3. The requester has disclosed the capacity in which they are acting.
4. The requester has authority to submit the request, if claiming to represent another party.
5. The submitted URLs identify the material actually being challenged.
6. The supporting evidence has not knowingly been manipulated to misrepresent the issue.
7. Material allegations are based on information the requester believes to be accurate.
8. Facts, assumptions, and opinions have been distinguished where relevant.
9. The stated legal or contractual basis has been identified accurately.
10. The requested action has been described accurately.
11. The request does not knowingly omit information that would materially change its meaning.
12. The requester understands that HyperZHub may independently evaluate the request.

The declaration should include the requester's printed legal name, role, date, and physical or electronic signature.

This declaration does not replace any declaration required by a particular statute or legal process.

## 2.15. Incomplete, Abusive, or Misleading Requests

HyperZHub may request clarification, additional evidence, or verification where appropriate.

Requests may be challenged or declined where they:

- Fail to identify the affected material.
- Contain no meaningful explanation.
- Misrepresent the requester's identity.
- Misrepresent organizational authority.
- Cite irrelevant or inapplicable laws.
- Provide materially misleading evidence.
- Demand unrelated material be removed.
- Seek a broader restriction without explaining its basis.
- Misrepresent an internal institutional policy as a binding legal order.
- Appear intended to intimidate, harass, or suppress lawful material without an adequate basis.

This section does not authorize HyperZHub to disregard binding legal process or applicable legal obligations.

## 2.16. Review and Verification

HyperZHub may:

1. Review the submission.
2. Verify the requester's identity.
3. Verify organizational contact information.
4. Request confirmation of authority.
5. Review supporting documents.
6. Examine the identified resources.
7. Assess the stated legal or contractual basis.
8. Evaluate the requested scope.
9. Request clarification.
10. Contact an organization through independently verifiable channels.
11. Seek professional legal advice where appropriate.
12. Take action required by applicable law.
13. Decline unsupported voluntary requests.

Submitting a request does not guarantee a particular response time or outcome.

## 2.17. Confidentiality and Personal Information

Requesters should submit only information reasonably relevant to their complaint.

HyperZHub may limit the handling of unnecessary personal information and may request redacted copies of documents where appropriate.

Do not send passwords, account credentials, financial account details, or unrelated sensitive personal information.

## 2.18. No Admission of Liability

Acknowledging, investigating, responding to, or voluntarily acting on a general takedown request does not automatically constitute an admission of liability, wrongdoing, or agreement with the requester's allegations.

## 2.19. Reservation of Rights

HyperZHub reserves the right to evaluate requests individually, verify submitted information, request supporting documentation, dispute unsupported allegations, and take actions permitted by applicable law.

Nothing in this policy waives rights or defenses available under applicable law.
